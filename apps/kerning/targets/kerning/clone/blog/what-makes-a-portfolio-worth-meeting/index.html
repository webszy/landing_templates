What makes a portfolio worth meeting

Ines CallowayScout and creative director18 Sep 2026·6 min read·Craft

worth meeting. calm, exact.

I review somewhere between twenty and forty portfolios a month. Most are competent. A few are beautiful. Only a handful make me want to write to a hiring team and say "you need to meet this person." Here is what usually makes the difference.

Decisions, not deliverables

Final screens tell me what shipped. They rarely tell me why. The portfolios that stand out walk through two or three moments where the designer had to choose, show what they gave up, and say plainly what they would do differently now.

A sentence like "we cut the dashboard entirely and moved the one number people needed into the weekly email" tells me more about someone's judgement than ten polished mockups.

Restraint

The best work often looks simple, and that is the trap. Simple is expensive. I look for evidence that a designer understood the complexity and chose not to pass it on to the user: edge cases handled quietly, empty states that actually help, copy that says one thing well.

Details nobody asked for

Every portfolio I have recommended had at least one detail that was clearly not in the brief. A loading state with a sense of humour. A table that respects its numbers. An error message written by someone who has been frustrated by error messages.

The details nobody asked for show you what someone cares about when nobody is checking.

What I mostly ignore

  • Process diagrams that could belong to any project.
  • Long lists of tools.
  • Logos, unless the work on that brand is actually shown.
  • Case studies longer than the project felt.

None of this is a checklist, and none of it replaces looking closely. But if you are putting your work together, show the thinking, cut what you would cut from a product, and leave in the one detail you are quietly proud of. Someone will notice.
